Water-Based vs. Plastisol Inks: Why We Only Use Water-Based

By Cowdog Print Co. • January 26, 2025 • 10 min read

Most screen printers use plastisol because it's easier. We use water-based because it's better. Here's the honest breakdown — no greenwashing, just facts about what you're actually getting printed on your shirts.

The short version: Plastisol sits on top of fabric like plastic. Water-based soaks in and becomes part of the fabric. One feels like a sticker, the other feels like the shirt itself. We chose water-based and we'll never go back.

What Actually IS the Difference?

Let's start with what these inks actually are — because most explanations make it more confusing than it needs to be.

Plastisol Ink: Liquid Plastic

What it is: PVC (polyvinyl chloride) particles suspended in a plasticizer. It's essentially liquid plastic that hardens when heated.

How it prints: Sits on top of the fabric as a separate layer. Cures at 320°F. Never dries on its own — stays wet in the screen until you heat it.

What it feels like: A raised, rubbery layer. You can feel exactly where the print is, especially on darker garments where thick underbase is needed.

Water-Based Ink: Dye in Water

What it is: Pigment particles suspended in water with binders. Think of it like a stain that bonds to the fabric fibers.

How it prints: Soaks into the fabric and bonds with the fibers. Cures at 320°F but starts drying immediately once printed.

What it feels like: You can't feel it. Run your hand over a water-based print and you feel the fabric texture, not a plastic layer.

The Touch Test

Find a vintage concert tee from the 80s or 90s. The print is cracked and faded but still soft as butter? That's water-based. Find a modern mall brand tee where the print feels like a vinyl sticker? That's plastisol.

Why Most Shops Use Plastisol (And Why We Don't)

Here's the truth: plastisol is easier to work with. About 95% of screen printing shops in the US use it exclusively. Here's why:

Plastisol Advantages (From a Printer's Perspective)

  • Never dries in the screen — Can sit for hours without clogging
  • Prints on anything — Works great on polyester, synthetic blends
  • Super opaque — Bright colors on dark garments with less ink
  • Forgiving — Easier to print, fewer variables to control
  • Consistent — Behaves the same every time, every season

The catch: All those advantages benefit the printer, not the customer. And definitely not the person wearing the shirt.

Why We Chose Water-Based Anyway

We started printing with plastisol. It's what everyone uses, it's what we learned on. Then we printed our first water-based job and immediately felt the difference.

The moment that changed everything: We printed a run of black tees with white designs. Plastisol version felt stiff and heavy. Water-based version felt like the design had always been part of the shirt. We wore both for a month. The water-based tee got softer and better. The plastisol tee started cracking.

We switched to 100% water-based the next month. That was 2022. We haven't looked back.

Real Talk

Yes, water-based is harder to work with. Screens dry out faster. Inks behave differently in humidity. We need more skill and attention. But that's our problem, not yours. You shouldn't have to wear a worse product because it's easier for us to print.

When Does Plastisol Actually Win?

Look, we're not dogmatic. There are specific cases where plastisol is the right choice:

Plastisol Makes Sense For:

  • 100% polyester garments — Water-based doesn't bond well to synthetic fibers
  • Performance athletic wear — Moisture-wicking synthetics need plastisol
  • Neon/fluorescent colors — True neons are only available in plastisol
  • Specialty inks — Puff ink, glow-in-the-dark, some metallics
  • Extremely high-volume orders — Thousands of pieces where speed matters most

Our solution: We print on cotton, cotton blends, and natural fibers. We avoid polyester. If you need neon or specialty effects, we'll refer you to a trusted plastisol printer we know does quality work.

Better to turn down a job than compromise on what we believe makes better prints.

The Environmental Angle (Without the BS)

Let's address the elephant in the room: Yes, water-based ink is better for the environment. But we don't lead with that because honestly? Most customers care more about how the shirt feels than the eco-friendliness.

And that's fine. We do too.

The Actual Environmental Differences:

Plastisol:

  • Contains PVC (polyvinyl chloride) — releases dioxins when produced and incinerated
  • Phthalate plasticizers (some linked to health concerns, now regulated)
  • Requires chemical cleaners for screen cleanup
  • Not biodegradable — it's plastic

Water-Based:

  • Water as the carrier (cleaned up with... water)
  • No PVC or phthalates
  • Biodegradable components (though binders vary)
  • Lower VOC (volatile organic compound) emissions

Our take: The environmental benefits are real, but they're a bonus. We chose water-based because it makes better prints. The eco-friendliness is nice, but it's not why we're here.

🌱

Honest Environmental Statement: Water-based ink is better for the planet than plastisol. But t-shirt production in general — cotton farming, dyeing, shipping — has way bigger environmental impact than ink choice. If you want truly eco-friendly merch, look at the whole supply chain, not just the ink.

What This Means for Your Order

If you're ordering from us (or any water-based printer), here's what to actually expect:

You'll Get:

  • Softer prints that feel like part of the shirt
  • Better breathability — especially important for all-over prints
  • Vintage aesthetic — that worn-in concert tee vibe from day one
  • Prints that age gracefully — they get softer, not cracked
  • Matte finish — no shiny plasticky look

Trade-Offs to Know About:

  • Less opaque on dark garments — colors might be slightly less bright than plastisol on black/navy
  • Better on natural fibers — won't work well on 100% polyester
  • Prints can fade slightly — like any natural dye, colors mellow over time (we think this looks better)
  • Requires proper garment care — wash inside-out, avoid high heat in dryer
Dark Garments & Water-Based Ink

Printing water-based ink on black shirts requires more skill than plastisol. We need thicker underbase, more precise registration, better technique. It's harder for us. But the result — a print that's still soft despite the underbase — is worth it.

How to Care for Water-Based Prints

Water-based prints are durable, but they'll last longer if you treat them right:

Best Practices:

  1. Wash inside-out — Reduces friction on the print surface
  2. Cold or warm water — Hot water fades any print faster
  3. Skip the dryer (or use low heat) — High heat breaks down ink binders over time
  4. Don't iron directly on print — Turn inside-out or use parchment paper
  5. Air dry when possible — Gentlest option for longevity

Real talk: I throw my water-based printed shirts in the washer and dryer on normal settings. They're fine. The care instructions above will maximize lifespan, but water-based prints aren't delicate flowers. They just benefit from the same care you'd give any quality garment.

How to Tell What Ink Your Shirt Has

Most shirts don't list ink type. Here's how to tell:

It's Probably Plastisol If:

  • You can feel a thick, raised layer
  • The print has a slight shine/glossy finish
  • Colors are super bright and opaque
  • Print feels stiff or rubbery
  • Cracks appear after washing

It's Probably Water-Based If:

  • You can feel the fabric texture through the print
  • Print has a matte, natural finish
  • Feels exactly like the rest of the shirt
  • Colors blend naturally with the fabric
  • Gets softer with each wash

The ultimate test: Close your eyes and run your hand across the print. If you can't tell where the design starts and stops, it's water-based.

Common Myths About Water-Based Ink

Myth #1: "Water-based prints fade faster"

Reality: They fade differently. Plastisol stays bright then suddenly starts cracking and peeling. Water-based mellows gradually and evenly, like a vintage tee. We think the faded look is part of the charm — but if you want bright colors forever, neither ink type will deliver that.

Myth #2: "Water-based can't print on dark shirts"

Reality: We print on black shirts constantly. It requires more skill (proper underbase technique) but absolutely works. Some shops avoid it because it's harder — that's a skill issue, not an ink issue.

Myth #3: "Water-based is way more expensive"

Reality: Ink costs are similar. Labor costs are slightly higher (it requires more attention), but our pricing is competitive with plastisol shops. You're not paying a premium for water-based at Cowdog.

Myth #4: "You can't get vibrant colors with water-based"

Reality: On light fabrics, water-based colors are incredibly vibrant. On dark fabrics, they're slightly less bright than plastisol — but still look great. The trade-off is worth it for the hand feel.
